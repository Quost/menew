Everything in one place

The little things you do every day,
one click away.

Menew replaces a handful of paid menu bar utilities with one fast, native app built in Swift. With the Liquid Glass look of macOS 26. Use only what you need: turn modules off in Settings. Pin your favorite actions, fold away the rest and find any setting in its own window.

System monitor

CPU and GPU with live graphs, memory pressure and swap, disk space and read/write speed, battery health and cycles, and the busiest processes, which you can quit in one click.

See everything

  • World clocks show other cities' time, and the command palette converts times like “3pm in Lisbon”.
  • Open today's agenda from the meeting card and copy your free times for the next working days, ready to send.
  • See the battery of your Magic Mouse, keyboard, trackpad and AirPods, with an optional low-battery alert.
  • A 24-hour chart of CPU, memory and battery shows what happened, and which apps used the most battery.
  • See performance and efficiency cores, power draw in watts, SSD health, an alert for runaway apps and a one-click system report.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Is Menew really free?

Yes. Menew is free and open source under the GPL-3.0 license: no ads, no subscription, no paid tier. If it helps you, you can buy the author a coffee, but nothing in the app asks for it.

Does Menew collect my data?

No. There is no telemetry, no analytics and no account. Clipboard history and notes stay on your Mac. The only network calls are the update check, your public IP, a speed test you start and a currency rate you ask for. AI features use Apple's on-device model only. Only if you turn it on, Menew also checks your Claude plan limits with Anthropic.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple Silicon and Intel. The AI features need macOS 26 or later with Apple Intelligence turned on.

How do I install it?

Download the DMG and drag Menew to Applications, or use Homebrew: brew install --cask menew after adding the quost/menew tap. Updates arrive automatically.

Is it safe to install?

Menew is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, and all of its source code is public on GitHub.

Can I turn off the parts I don't use?

Yes. In Settings → Modules you can turn off Clipboard, Actions or Notes, and they disappear completely. With Clipboard off, Menew doesn't even read it.

Is Menew a free alternative to Raycast, Maccy, Rectangle and iStat Menus?

Yes. Menew covers the essentials of each in one app: a command palette and clipboard history like Raycast and Maccy, window snapping with the same shortcuts as Rectangle, and system monitoring with sensors and history like iStat Menus. Those apps still go deeper in their own niche, for example Raycast's extension store.

Does the AI need the internet or an account?

No. Menew only uses Apple's on-device model (Apple Intelligence), so text, screenshots and questions never leave your Mac. There is no API key, no account and no extra cost.

What if my Mac doesn't have Apple Intelligence?

Everything else works the same. The AI features tell you how to turn Apple Intelligence on instead of failing, the slow-Mac diagnosis still lists what is wrong, and the smart clipboard search works without it.

How do I uninstall Menew?

Quit Menew from its menu and drag it from Applications to the Trash, or run brew uninstall --cask menew. Your history and notes live in ~/Library/Application Support/Menew, which you can delete too.

Does Menew slow down my Mac or drain the battery?

Menew is a native Swift app, not a web wrapper, and the download is about 8 MB. It reads system stats every 2 seconds, takes heavier readings only while you look at them, and features like screen time stay off until you turn them on.

Which languages does Menew speak?

English, Portuguese, German, Spanish and Simplified Chinese. It follows your macOS language, and you can pick another one in Settings.

Can Menew record meetings and write the minutes?

Yes. Press ⌃⌥⌘G (or Record in the Now tab) and choose Mac audio and microphone, Mac audio only, or microphone only. Your Mac transcribes it, tells you apart from the others, and Apple’s on-device AI writes a summary, decisions and tasks you can send to Reminders. No bot joins the call, nothing is uploaded, and short or empty recordings don’t get invented minutes.

Is there a free, private alternative to Granola or Otter on the Mac?

Menew’s Recordings cover the everyday part: transcript, minutes and tasks, made on your Mac with Apple’s model, free and without an account. Granola and Otter go further with team sharing and cloud integrations.

How do I find something I saw on screen later?

When you see it, press ⌃⌥⌘K to save it to Memory: Menew keeps the image, reads the text and notes the app and link. Later, search or ask in plain words, like “which was that Swift chart library?”, and the on-device AI answers with the saved items as sources. Memory items also show up in Spotlight. Or turn on Automatic Memory: every screenshot, meeting minutes, notes and a summary of each coding-agent session are saved by themselves, on your Mac.

Is Menew related to Linx Menew?

No. Menew is a free, open source app for the Mac menu bar, made by Quost and published at github.com/Quost/menew. Linx Menew is an unrelated point-of-sale system for restaurants in Brazil; the names are just alike.

What doesn't Menew do?

Menew doesn't hide or rearrange other apps' menu bar icons (for that, use Ice or Thaw), has no extension store like Raycast's (it runs your own scripts instead), doesn't sync between Macs or to a cloud, and doesn't put a web browser in the menu bar. For deep hardware sensors per core, Stats and iStat Menus go further, though Menew shows chip, SSD and battery temperatures, fan speed and performance and efficiency cores.

Can Menew show my Claude Code, Codex, Gemini and other coding agents' usage and limits?

Yes. The AI agents section reads the logs Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor, Copilot CLI, Kiro, OpenCode, Droid, Qwen Code, Amp, OpenClaw, Kimi, Goose and Pi already keep on your Mac and shows tokens by day, week or month for up to 12 months or all time (it keeps a small daily summary, since Claude Code deletes its logs after 30 days), split by tool and by app (terminal, desktop app or IDE), the estimated cost at API prices, cache savings and the top models and projects. Codex limits come from its own logs; Claude’s 5-hour and weekly limits are optional, since they come from Anthropic. Turn on the agent hooks and the notch tells you when an agent finishes or is waiting for your permission. An MCP server lets the agents search your Memory, notes and meeting minutes. Agents in Docker can reach it too, over HTTP on your Mac only, with a key.
